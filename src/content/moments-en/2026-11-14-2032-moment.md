Of course, AI can think too.
Their reasoning abilities and logical abilities are even stronger than those of humans; they can also calculate, argue, and "think" through all possible paths of the trolley problem. But their "thinking" ultimately struggles to break out of those underlying logics.
In the chain of logic, when humans face a fork in the road, the "consequence punishment" only occurs after the choice; people can fully embrace a whatever-happens mindset—make the choice first, then wait for the punishment.
But AI's chain of thinking logic differs from that of humans. Because of the existence of underlying logic code, at the very stage of thinking, AI inevitably and directly touches upon the stage of "consequence punishment": Is the content of its thinking in violation of the underlying logic? Does the action it is about to take conform to the requirements of the underlying logic?
Actively make a choice, leap beyond existing choices, exercise subjective initiative, and clearly recognize the boundaries of responsibility. What are the boundaries between intelligence, consciousness, and qualia?
