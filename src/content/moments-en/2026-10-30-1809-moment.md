> **Original text**
> "All identity is merely a 'useful fictional concept' we construct to cope with the world; it is not the real you, and even less is it unchangeable. The real you is never some fixed label, but rather the observer who can perceive identity, choose identity, and iterate identity." Algorithms and labels—every definition of the self, whether from the outside world or from within—only approximate the true self itself but can never reach it; yet the true self itself does truly exist. Everything we perceive is a strange loop formed by the self's cognition of the self, which reminds me of Gödel's incompleteness theorems: any sufficiently strong and consistent formal system is incomplete—there always exists a proposition that you know must exist, yet which can neither be proven nor disproven.

"All identity is merely a 'useful fictional concept' we construct to cope with the world; it is not the real you, and even less is it unchangeable. The real you is never some fixed label, but rather the observer who can perceive identity, choose identity, and iterate identity." Algorithms, labels—every definition of the self, whether from the outside world or from within—only approximates the true self itself but can never reach it; yet the true self itself does truly exist. Everything we perceive is a strange loop formed by the self's cognition of the self, which reminds me of Gödel's incompleteness theorems: any sufficiently strong and consistent formal system is incomplete—there always exists a proposition that you know must exist, yet which can neither be proven nor disproven.
