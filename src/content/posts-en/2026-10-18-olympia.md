You want to seriously discuss a question—"Does free will exist?" "Should I quit my job to prepare for the postgraduate entrance exam?"—but after scrolling through your entire contact list, you cannot find anyone willing to spar with you. You ask AI, and it says: "You're absolutely right. That is indeed a question worth pondering."

Agreement is the mortal enemy of thought. In 1902, Einstein, then a junior patent-office clerk, and two friends spontaneously formed the "Olympia Academy" in Bern: they read Mach, Hume, and Poincaré together, then argued fiercely. No authority, no degrees, no audience—only a friendship built on "cornering the question." Three years later, special relativity was born.

**Olympia** aims to recreate that **mechanism and atmosphere**: a local-first multi-agent debate system. You propose a topic, and several "fellows" with different positions engage you under a formal debate protocol; every argument is recorded, distilled, and tracked across sessions. It is currently at P0.5—a minimal CLI validation, testing the make-or-break hypothesis first: **can AI produce genuine dissent rather than polite additions?**

## Core Idea: Dissent Comes from Cognitive Division of Labor, Not Personality Acting

**Three constitutional design principles** (non-negotiable):

1. **Dissent before agreement**—agreement is this product's failure state.
2. **Argument before eloquence**—every statement must be decomposed into a testable structure of "claim—warrant—evidence—qualifier—rebuttal"; rhetoric cannot substitute for reasons.
3. **Memory before single-turn dialogue**—academy sessions are continuous, and stance snapshots and unresolved disagreements are persisted. This is its fundamental difference from a chat window.

**Functional seats, not celebrity role-play.** The Agents do not impersonate Einstein himself—that would degenerate into accent imitation and a collage of famous quotations. Each seat represents a cognitive function:

| Seat | Cognitive function | Mandatory rule |
|------|----------|----------|
| Builder | Propose bold, unifying explanations | Must offer a falsifiable proposition; merely discussing "possibilities" is forbidden |
| Skeptic | Find counterexamples and check boundary conditions | At least one concrete counterexample or "if this holds, we should see X" per round |
| Questioner | Conceptual clarification | Takes no position and only asks questions; every question must target a specific statement |
| Scribe | Structured minutes | Does not debate; only performs faithful compression and traceable citation |
| Devil's advocate (rotating) | Defend the weakest side | Mandatorily assigned whenever consensus is about to form |

**The protocol library is the real moat.** Anyone can write an Agent framework; the unique asset is the first six built-in debate protocols: **steelman opening** (before rebutting, restate the other side's argument in the strongest form they would endorse), **blind-position rounds** (independently submit positions before seeing one another's content, preventing groupthink), **Toulmin skeleton** (a JSON Schema constrains output to five fields), **Socratic questioning** (ask only one question at a time until the definition collapses), **thesis—antithesis—synthesis** (the synthesis must state exactly what it absorbed from the antithesis; false balance is forbidden), and **ceasefire rules** (unresolved disagreements are entered into a pending-case ledger; no pretending they have been resolved).

## Implementation Steps

**1. CLI first, UI later.** The kernel is testable and replayable without an interface, so interface development cannot hold it back. Three commands complete the loop:

```bash
pnpm olympia debate "自由意志是否存在" --rounds 3   # Convene an academy session
pnpm olympia replay transcripts/<file>.json         # Replay
pnpm olympia minutes transcripts/<file>.json        # View minutes
```

**2. A deterministic state machine, with the LLM generating only at nodes.** The flow must be code (testable, replayable, evaluable); the model is responsible only for generating content at round nodes. It uses a **centralized director mode**: only the core engine can call models and advance rounds, and Agents cannot spawn one another—this prevents the most common failures of multi-agent systems: self-indulgent loops, token explosions, and topic drift.

**3. Cost control built in.** Model tiers are assigned by seat (a strong model for the Builder, a cheap model for the Scribe), rounds have a hard cap, each academy session displays tokens and estimated cost, and blind-position rounds use concurrent calls to save wall-clock time.

**4. P0.5 spike: hit the make-or-break question first.** The entire project's life-or-death question is "quality of dissent"—if the AI fellows can only find new ways to agree, everything that comes later is pointless. So the first milestone includes no UI and no memory; it only runs real academy sessions through the CLI, using five sets of topic fixtures + an acceptance rubric to test whether the dissent holds up. Two real debate rounds on "Does free will exist?" have now been completed, leaving full transcripts for human acceptance review. Once they pass, the project moves to P1 (yearbook memory, stance tracking).

## Ideas It Draws On

- **The history of the Olympia Academy** (Bern, 1902–1905): the reading group of Einstein, Solovine, and Habicht—the source of the project's name and atmosphere, recreating the mechanism rather than the people.
- **The Toulmin model of argument** (Stephen Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument*): the five fields of claim/warrant/evidence/qualifier/rebuttal are the executable form of "argument before eloquence."
- **The Socratic method**: continuous questioning until definitions collapse—the prototype for the Questioner seat.
- **Dialectics** (thesis—antithesis—synthesis): three mandatory rounds of progression, with pseudo-synthesis forbidden.
- **The steelman principle** (Rapoport's rules, popularized by Dennett in *Intuition Pumps*): restate the other side's position to their satisfaction before attacking it—an engineered defense against the strawman fallacy.
- **Deliberative democracy and adversarial collaboration**: dissent is not noise but a mechanism—as in the AnimaEVA article, this project also translates cognitive science into runnable system constraints.

## Current Status and What Remains

The code is complete and merged into main (debate engine, CLI, Anthropic provider, replay, and minutes). Still to come: full acceptance across the five topics → P1 yearbook memory (SQLite event sourcing + stance snapshots) → a Tauri desktop app. This is an "unfinished" project, but once the make-or-break validation passes, it will grow quickly.
