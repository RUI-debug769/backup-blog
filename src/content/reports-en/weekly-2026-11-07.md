AI Technology & Ideas Weekly · No.03
WEEKLY · 2026.11.07
==================================

[This Week's Headline] Frontier Pacing: The AI Industry Collectively Hits the Brakes for the First Time
This week, Dario Amodei's call to slow down received rare endorsements from Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis, while METR data shows AI itself contributes roughly 80% of capability acceleration; on one side the capability curve steepens, on the other the industry forms a consensus on pacing for the first time, compounded by the market expressing doubts about returns on capital expenditure through downward financing revisions and sharp stock declines—AI is shifting from a racing narrative to one of pacing and revaluation.

----------------------------------

[Concept 01] Frontier Pacing
Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier" this week, arguing that model capability improvement has outpaced researchers' understanding and control, and calling for actively slowing the frontier pace; Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Demis Hassabis, and Satya Nadella rarely endorsed this publicly, while Donald Trump rebutted it and emphasized maintaining the lead. METR's November 5 report provided the quantified other side: Anthropic's AI capability improvement rate is roughly twice as fast as before, with no clear bottleneck visible over the coming year.
Why it matters: This marks the embryonic form of the industry shifting from a "racing" narrative to a "pacing" consensus—even if motivations differ, frontier labs acknowledge for the first time collectively that speed itself is a variable requiring governance. It foreshadows that subsequent regulatory discourse, safety investment, and model release cadence may all revolve around "pacing" rather than simply "leading."

[Concept 02] Decentralized Agent Swarms
At ADIPEC 2026, Sam Altman disclosed that the next-generation GPT drove roughly 10,000 agents to complete a Navier–Stokes mathematical breakthrough in about 88 hours, emphasizing that the system has "no hierarchy, no boss, completely decentralized." Meanwhile, DeepSeek open-sourced the Super-Agent prototype, adopting a two-tier structure of "supervisor layer dispatching and accepting, worker layer executing in parallel," which can discover repository bugs and submit fixes end-to-end in about 15 minutes.
Why it matters: Two approaches—full decentralization and supervisor-worker hierarchy—are defining the new question of "how AI labor is organized," replacing the previous generation's single-model, single-task paradigm. It suggests that future system capability ceilings may depend on coordination mechanism design, not merely model parameter scale.

[Concept 03] Recursive Self-Improvement Enters the Empirical Stage (Recursive Self-Improvement, Measured)
Based on Anthropic's internal data from August 12 to October 29, METR estimates that its AI capability improvement rate is roughly twice as fast as before, with AI itself contributing about 80% of the acceleration; Anthropic's revised S-1 also warns that recursive self-improvement (RSI) capability is nearing a breakthrough. This week's market attention to RSI was also reflected in: after rumors of an "RSI startup," Google DeepMind researcher Yao Shunyu publicly denied founding such a company.
Why it matters: RSI is shifting from a theoretical concern to an estimable, disclosable empirical variable, entering the视野 of IPO filings and third-party assessment institutions. This means the feedback loop of "AI accelerating AI R&D" is beginning to have data to rely on, and safety discussions will shift from philosophical debate to engineering and measurement levels.

[Concept 04] Inference Economics / Token Factory
OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra pushed agent task costs at medium inference intensity down to about $0.29, roughly one-sixteenth of GPT-6 Astra; inference infrastructure company Fal doubled its valuation to $16 billion in half a year and received strategic investment from NVIDIA; Moore Threads, Qujing Technology, and Zhipu jointly released the "Domestic Heterogeneous Inference AI Token Factory Solution."
Why it matters: The industry's value center of gravity is shifting from frontier training to cost per token and throughput, and the inference/deployment layer is beginning to absorb the largest incremental capital. For enterprises and developers, model selection will increasingly be determined by service economics (cost-effectiveness, available compute) rather than capability leaderboards alone.

[Idea 05] Agents in the Transaction Layer
Anthropic launched a Personal Finance beta for Claude, which can connect to bank accounts via Plaid in read-only mode to analyze spending; Airbnb's CEO announced plans to launch an AI travel agent in the first half of 2027 that can assist with rebooking and resolving issues during stays; more than a quarter of Meta's Muse usage is shopping-related, and Mark Zuckerberg said he plans to charge merchants for transactions completed through Muse; OpenAI reached a cybersecurity-focused strategic partnership with JPMorganChase.
Why it matters: Multiple signals point to the same speculation: agents are crossing from "reading information" into the transition zone of "handling funds"—first establishing trust with read-only permissions, then evolving toward transactions and fees. Whoever becomes the trusted transaction entry point in the agent era will control the next distribution layer.

[Implementation Path] Sparse-MoE Open Weights
This week, multiple models simultaneously chose the MoE approach of "large total parameters + small activated parameters" and opened their weights: Kimi K3.1 Pro has 2.8 trillion total parameters and about 104 billion activated (weights released around November 4); Mistral Large 4 has 1.05 trillion total parameters and about 52 billion activated per task; Baidu ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base has 280 billion total parameters and 14 billion activated per token; Thinking Machines Inkling 2 Small has 594 billion total parameters and about 17 billion activated, planned for release under Apache 2.0. The key components are sparse expert routing and low activation ratios, enabling ultra-large models to serve under limited VRAM and compute; current maturity is at the stage of "weights downloadable, ecosystem toolchains still catching up."
Why it matters: This path is turning "open weights approaching the closed-source frontier" from a slogan into an engineerable default path, significantly lowering the self-deployment threshold for developers and enterprises.

[Implementation Path] Engineering Combinations for Lower Inference Costs (Quantization + Context Offloading + Heterogeneous Hardware)
NVIDIA released AI memory full-stack co-design: using NVFP4 compression, NVLink allocation, and context offloading to reduce HBM usage, claiming that under specific DeepSeek V4 Pro workloads, Vera Rubin NVL72 delivers up to 30x higher throughput per dollar than GB300 NVL72; on the domestic side, Moore Threads, Qujing Technology, and Zhipu jointly released the "Domestic Heterogeneous Inference AI Token Factory Solution." The key components are low-precision quantization formats, KV/context offloading, and heterogeneous chip scheduling; in terms of current maturity, NVFP4 and offloading solutions are at the release/early deployment stage, and the domestic heterogeneous solution has just completed joint release.
Why it matters: These engineering approaches collectively determine "inference throughput per dollar," the last mile in converting model capability into sustainable commercial services.
